The Human Connection Factors:

Communication style in plain language: Do they explain things like I'm a colleague, a child, or something in between? Do they use medical jargon or translate everything?
Emotional approach: Are they the "fierce warrior" type, the "calm navigator," or the "empathetic hand-holder"? I need to know if they'll cry with me or be my bulldog.
Availability during crisis: Will they answer at 10pm when I get scary test results? What's their actual response time?

The Practical Stuff Nobody Lists:

What they've actually fought for and won: Not just "insurance denials" but specific stories - "Got a $47K experimental treatment approved" or "Found a specialist who takes Medicaid after 3 tries failed"
Their stamina level: Will they stick with me through a 6-month appeal process or do they typically work in short bursts?
How they handle conflict: Do they go straight to escalation or try diplomatic routes first? Some patients need someone to storm the gates; others need finesse.

The Trust Indicators:

Have they been a patient themselves or a caregiver? This fundamentally changes how they understand urgency and fear.
What they DON'T do: Clear boundaries help me understand if they're right for my needs
Their network strength: Do they have actual relationships with specialists, social workers, billing departments? Or will they be cold-calling like I would?

Decision-Making Philosophy:

How much do they push their opinion vs. present options? I want to know if they'll respect me saying "no more chemo" or if they'll try to convince me otherwise.
How they handle medical complexity: Do they get energized by rare diseases and research deep-dives, or do they prefer straightforward cases?

The Uncomfortable Questions:

What cases do they turn down and why? This tells me so much about fit.
Have they ever quit on a patient? Under what circumstances?
What frustrates them about patients? (People who don't follow through? People who are too passive? I need to know if I'll annoy them.)
